You run a pub

Each week customers come in, and each wants one keg of beer. You serve them from your cellar and order more from your wholesaler. One mug on screen is one keg.

Before you start. Anyone can play the game. To do the exercise that follows it, you need the basics of stock-and-flow diagrams: what a stock is, what a flow is, and how information links connect them. This app gives you practice in using them. It does not teach them from the beginning.

Choose a game
  • Your wholesaler gets your order at once and tells you what it can send. Delivery then takes time: kegs you order now reach you three weeks later, if the wholesaler has them.
  • A keg sitting in your cellar costs £0.50 a week.
  • A customer you cannot serve waits. Each keg you owe a customer costs £1 a week, and you still have to supply it.
  • These are the only two costs. Placing an order is free, and so are the kegs themselves.
  • Your one decision each week is how many kegs to order. Keep your total cost low.
  • The game runs for up to 50 weeks.
  • Your wholesaler gets your order at once and tells you what it can send. Delivery then takes time: kegs you order now reach you three weeks later, if the wholesaler has them.
  • Your cellar holds 20 kegs. Kegs from a delivery that do not fit are sent back.
  • A customer you cannot serve does not wait. They leave, and they tell others.
  • Word gets around. A pub that serves everyone draws a bigger crowd. A pub that turns people away draws a smaller one.
  • There is no money in this game. Your one decision each week is how many kegs to order. Serve as many customers as you can.
  • The game runs for up to 50 weeks.
